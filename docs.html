Mental Model

The 6-Step APCAF Evaluation Chain

What is APCAF in Plain English?

APCAF is an open specification to check whether physical security controls resist known adversary bypasses. Instead of asking only "Is there a lock?", APCAF asks "What physical condition would permit bypass, and does the installed hardware prevent that condition?"

01. TACTIC
Security Objective
Portal Protection: Safeguard physical access portals against unauthenticated ingress.
02. HYPOTHESIS
Bypass Path
Latch Manipulation (PHY-T1002): Mechanical manipulation of latch bolt via exposed frame gap.
03. OBSERVATION
Physical Check
Feeler Gauge: Measure frame strike clearance against NFPA 80 (3.2mm).
04. EVIDENCE
Logged Record
Measured Gap: 4.5mm clearance with photo evidence and gauge serial.
05. ASSESSMENT
Disposition
Deficient: Frame margin exceeds allowable clearance without protective astragal.
06. REMEDIATION
Correction
PHY-M1002: Mount continuous steel astragal overlapping door stop.
Architecture Overview

APCAF Specification & Philosophy

The Adversarial Physical Control Assessment Framework (APCAF) is an open specification for standardized, non-invasive physical security control assessment. Drawing architectural inspiration from MITRE ATT&CK behavioral taxonomy principles, APCAF establishes objective evaluation criteria for physical and hardware-layer security.

Traditional compliance audits verify nominal presence on paper (confirming a lock or badge reader exists). APCAF standardizes non-invasive inspection to evaluate whether installed hardware withstands known bypass mechanisms through repeatable, non-destructive field procedures.

Framework Governance

What APCAF Does and Does NOT Claim

APCAF Does:

• Map adversarial techniques to observable physical hardware conditions.
• Define standardized, repeatable non-invasive inspection procedures.
• Document susceptibility findings and building code non-conformances.
• Support remediation verification and quality assurance tracking.

APCAF Does NOT:

• Perform destructive penetration testing or lockpicking.
• Prove successful exploitation or claim a demonstrated physical breach.
• Certify regulatory compliance (PCI DSS, ISO 27001, SOC 2 belong to formal audits).
• Guarantee that a physical control cannot be bypassed by other methods.
• Determine contractual liability or replace legal counsel.

Operational Authorization

Sample Assessment Authorization Language

APCAF inspections require zero destructive tools, zero lockpicks, and zero tampering with life-safety hardware. To document authorization in service agreements or audit memos, organizations may adapt this sample language: 

"Client explicitly authorizes the assessment team to perform non-invasive,
passive radio frequency (RF) credential reads and non-intrusive passive
physical network port link detection during the inspection to verify vendor
hardware installation specifications."

Notice: This is a sample operational template, not legal advice. Organizations should adapt it with qualified legal counsel.

Field Methodology

The Fast-Path Benchmark Execution Model

The APCAF Fast-Path Profile defines target inspection budgets for baseline checks during physical walks:

5 Seconds
RF Credential Read: Contactless pocket reader scan to verify AES-128 crypto handshake vs unencrypted static UID.
15 Seconds
Door & Latch Inspection: Feeler gauge check for frame strike clearance (≤ 3.2mm) and astragal guard presence.
15 Seconds
REX Sensor Placement: Optical line-of-sight check for interior REX PIR sensor detection field and deflector hood.
10 Seconds
Exposed Port Signal Check: Passive zero-packet LED link-state test to verify administrative port shutdown or isolation.
PHY-TAC-02: Credential Protection

PHY-T1001: Credential Technology Assessment

Base Coverage
Assessment Hypothesis

Static, unauthenticated credential identifiers may permit credential replay or cloning where the access-control architecture does not provide cryptographic mutual authentication.

The non-invasive check determines whether the credential operates on an unencrypted carrier (such as 125 kHz Low Frequency Prox) or broadcasts a static Card Serial Number (CSN) without cryptographic challenge-response authentication.

APCAF Assessment Specification (YAML)
id: "PHY-T1001"
name: "Credential Technology Assessment"
tactic_id: "PHY-TAC-02"
target_object: "BADGE"
target_time_seconds: 5
hypothesis: "Static unauthenticated identifiers may permit replay where mutual crypto is absent."
assessment_procedure:
  method: "passive_rf_interrogation"
  tools_required: ["Pocket dual-frequency RFID reader"]
  steps:
    - "Hold credential near reader."
    - "Identify carrier frequency (125 kHz vs 13.56 MHz)."
    - "Verify if ISO/IEC 14443-4 AES-128 handshake is initiated."
result_model:
  hardened_criteria: "Mutual AES-128 crypto smartcard (DESFire EV3 / Seos / PIV)."
  deficient_criteria: "Unencrypted 125 kHz Prox or static unauthenticated CSN."
limitations:
  can_establish: ["Carrier frequency and static UID broadcast exposure."]
  cannot_establish: ["Local cloning tool availability or secondary PIN/biometric policies."]
Remediation Guidance (PHY-M1001)

Upgrade credential stock to ISO/IEC 14443-4 smartcards with AES-128 mutual authentication (DESFire EV3 / Seos) and activate SAM cryptographic profiles on access readers.

PHY-TAC-03: Portal Protection

PHY-T1002: Door & Latch Protection Assessment

Base Coverage
Assessment Hypothesis

Door and frame geometry may permit mechanical manipulation of the latch bolt assembly where protective physical shielding is absent and strike clearances exceed engineering tolerances.

Building & Hardware Code Citations

• NFPA 80 §6.3.1.7.1: Clearances along top and vertical edges shall not exceed 1/8 in. (3.18 mm / 3.2 mm) for steel fire doors.
• ANSI/SDI A250.8 / SDI-122: Standard operating clearance of 1/8 in. (3.2 mm) for hollow metal door assemblies. 

id: "PHY-T1002"
name: "Door & Latch Protection Assessment"
tactic_id: "PHY-TAC-03"
target_object: "DOOR"
target_time_seconds: 15
assessment_procedure:
  method: "visual_and_feeler_gauge"
  tools_required: ["Feeler gap gauge", "Flashlight"]
result_model:
  hardened_criteria: "Continuous full-height steel astragal overlapping frame stop."
  deficient_criteria: "Strike clearance > 3.2mm with exposed latch bolt and no astragal."
limitations:
  can_establish: ["Perimeter strike clearance and protective guard coverage."]
  cannot_establish: ["Lock cylinder pin resistance or interior deadbolt status."]
Remediation Guidance (PHY-M1002)

Mount continuous full-height stainless steel security astragal plates overlapping the door frame stop on the exterior approach side.

PHY-TAC-03: Portal Protection

PHY-T1003: REX Sensor Placement Assessment

Base Coverage
Assessment Hypothesis

Interior Request-to-Exit (REX) motion detectors whose detection cones extend to perimeter door margins may be susceptible to non-contact activation through door seams if directional shielding is absent.

Remediation Guidance (PHY-M1003)

Mount UL-listed directional deflector hoods over interior REX PIR detectors (NFPA 101 §7.2.1.6.2) and install perimeter brush sweeps.

PHY-TAC-05: Interface Protection

PHY-T1004: Exposed Physical Network Interface Assessment

Base Coverage
Assessment Hypothesis

Accessible network jacks that maintain an active physical link may provide unauthorized network connectivity if administrative port shutdown, 802.1X, or MAC-based quarantine is misconfigured.

Observable Condition vs. Limitation

• Observable Condition: Publicly accessible RJ-45 drop exhibits active physical Layer 1 carrier signaling upon passive connection.
• Limitations: Does not prove successful IEEE 802.1X authentication, IP reachability, or network penetration.

Remediation Guidance (PHY-M1004)

Administratively disable unassigned switch ports (`shutdown`) at the patch panel and enforce IEEE 802.1X port-security controls.

Optional Profile

Vendor Remediation & Warranty Workflow (APCAF-WF-01)

APCAF-WF-01 is an optional commercial workflow extension for facility commissioning and contractor warranty punch list management:

Phase 1
Scope Authorization: Incorporate sample authorization language into engagement agreements.
Phase 2
Site Inspection: Evaluate credential cryptography, door clearances, and port carrier states.
Phase 3
Notice Delivery: Generate formal remediation punch list for general contractor review under applicable contract terms.
Phase 4
Verification Re-Test: Conduct verification walk to validate hardened remediation.

Open Specification

Governance & Contribution Process

APCAF is maintained by the APCAF Maintainers under an open RFC process:

Technique Submission Requirements

1. Non-invasive procedure: Defined non-destructive physical measurement.
2. Zero life-safety interference: No tampering with emergency egress or fire alarms.
3. Building/fire code citations: Verifiable industry standards (NFPA, ANSI/SDI, ISO/IEC).

Audit Scope & Consent Clause

Single-Sentence Fast-Path Clause

"Client explicitly authorizes the assessment team to perform non-invasive, passive radio frequency (RF) credential reads and non-intrusive passive physical network port link detection during the inspection to verify vendor hardware installation specifications."

Scope & Life-Safety Protection Guarantees

• Zero Operational Impact: Strictly non-intrusive reads only. No packet injection or sniffing.
• Life-Safety Non-Interference: Zero manipulation of fire alarm, emergency egress, or crash bar hardware.
• Contractual Purpose: Generates a quality assurance scorecard to assist client in validating contractor deliverables.
