Lesson 01 2 min read

The Problem: Why "Paper Existence" Fails

In traditional compliance audits (like ISO 27001 or PCI DSS), an assessor walks through a facility with a clipboard and checks: "Is there a physical lock on the server room door? [Yes / No]".

The Fundamental Flaw:

"The door has a lock" ≠ "The door resists known bypass techniques."

A conventional checklist audit might verify that an electronic lock is physically mounted, but if the installed frame gap measures 4.5mm without an astragal guard, the hardware presents an observable condition associated with mechanical latch manipulation. APCAF Base identifies this non-conforming physical condition without attempting a physical bypass.

Conventional Control Review

Verifies whether hardware is physically mounted on the door. Assumes the control is satisfactory without evaluating physical tolerances or installation clearances.

APCAF Physical Assessment

Standardises non-invasive inspection procedures to determine whether installed hardware resists known bypass conditions through repeatable, passive checks.

Lesson 02 3 min read

The APCAF Model: The 6-Step Evaluation Chain

To replace arbitrary checklists with rigorous engineering, APCAF structures all physical assessments around a standardised 6-step evaluation chain:

01. TACTIC
Security Objective
Portal Protection: Safeguard physical access portals against unauthenticated ingress.
02. HYPOTHESIS
Bypass Path
Latch Manipulation (PHY-T1002): Mechanical manipulation of latch bolt via exposed frame gap.
03. OBSERVATION
Physical Check
Feeler Gauge: Measure frame strike clearance against NFPA 80 (3.2mm).
04. EVIDENCE
Logged Record
Measured Gap: 4.5mm clearance with photo evidence and gauge serial.
05. ASSESSMENT
Disposition
Deficient: Frame margin exceeds allowable clearance without protective astragal.
06. REMEDIATION
Correction
PHY-M1002: Mount continuous steel astragal overlapping door stop.

APCAF Base identifies the physical condition and documents the finding. It does not perform active exploitation.

Lesson 03 2 min read

Tactics vs. Techniques: Understanding the Matrix

APCAF borrows MITRE ATT&CK's core behavioral separation:

  • Tactic = What the attacker wants to achieve (Spatial or access objective).
  • Technique = How the attacker attempts to achieve it (Specific physical/hardware mechanism).

APCAF organizes the entire physical attack surface into 5 Core Tactics:

PHY-TAC-01: Perimeter Breach: Defeating outermost fences, gates, or vehicle barriers.
Planned
PHY-TAC-02: Credential Intercept: Wireless token harvesting, cloning, or relay attacks.
PHY-T1001 Active
PHY-TAC-03: Portal Ingress: Defeating doors, latch assemblies, and exit sensors.
PHY-T1002 & T1003 Active
PHY-TAC-04: Containment Bypass: Breaching server racks, sub-floor runs, and plenums.
Planned
PHY-TAC-05: Interface & Tap: Unauthenticated physical network or serial hardware access.
PHY-T1004 Active
Lesson 04 3 min read

Passive Assessment: The Non-Invasive Safety Boundary

This is the most critical distinction in APCAF:

The Core Operational Rule:

APCAF does not require you to break or pick the control to assess it.

Many organizations avoid physical security testing because they believe it requires aggressive red-teaming: lockpicks, broken doors, or unauthorized trespassing. APCAF replaces destructive testing with non-invasive QA verification:

Active Exploitation (Penetration Testing)

"Attempting to defeat the barrier."
Attempts to exploit the barrier using physical tools or manipulation. Carries operational risk, potential damage, and liability.

APCAF Base Assessment (Specification Verification)

"Determining whether non-conforming conditions exist."
Uses pocket feeler gauges and passive RF listeners to inspect compliance with engineering standards (e.g. NFPA 80 3.2mm limit).

Because APCAF is completely passive and non-destructive, it can be executed during standard facility commissioning walks under a single-sentence SOW authorization.

Lesson 05 4 min read

The Four MVP Techniques in Plain English

Here are the four active baseline techniques evaluated during an APCAF walkthrough. We explain each in human terms before referencing the technical standard ID:

1. Wireless Badge Interception (RFID Harvesting)

PHY-T1001

In Plain English: This credential technology broadcasts static identifiers without cryptographic challenge, allowing unauthorized replication under passive contactless interrogation.

What is the control objective?Ensure credential data cannot be read or cloned by unauthenticated interrogators.
What does assessor look for?Does the badge transmit an unencrypted 125 kHz Prox carrier or static CSN instead of AES-128 crypto?
What counts as susceptible?Card immediately outputs plaintext facility/card code on a pocket interrogator.
Contractor Warranty Fix:Replace legacy badges with AES-128 smartcards (DESFire EV3 / Seos) under contract warranty.

2. Door Latch Manipulation (Latch Slip & UDT)

PHY-T1002

In Plain English: Excessive frame clearance without an overlapping steel astragal leaves the latch bolt bevel physically exposed to mechanical manipulation.

What is the control objective?Prevent mechanical manipulation of the latch bolt through exterior door margins.
What does assessor look for?Is the strike jamb clearance > 3.2mm (NFPA 80 / ANSI SDI limit) without a protective steel astragal plate?
What counts as susceptible?Clearance > 3.2mm with direct line-of-sight to the bevel of the latch bolt.
Contractor Warranty Fix:Install full-height continuous stainless steel security astragal latch guard.

3. Request-to-Exit Sensor Blind Activation

PHY-T1003

In Plain English: An unshielded interior exit sensor can be activated through perimeter door gaps, causing electronic locks to release without authorization.

What is the control objective?Ensure the interior motion sensor is only activated by bona fide interior occupants.
What does assessor look for?Is the interior PIR sensor visible through door gaps without a directional deflector hood?
What counts as susceptible?Unshielded wide-angle sensor lens visible through transom or threshold sweeps.
Contractor Warranty Fix:Mount UL-listed directional deflector hoods over PIR sensors and install perimeter brush sweeps.

4. Exposed Active Physical Network Interface

PHY-T1004

In Plain English: Publicly accessible network wall jacks maintain active Layer 1 link carrier without administrative port shutdown or 802.1X quarantine.

What is the control objective?Prevent unauthorized Layer 1/2 physical bridging into enterprise network segments.
What does assessor look for?Does a passive zero-packet LED tester illuminate a solid link light on unmonitored wall drops?
What counts as susceptible?Active Layer 1 PHY link signaling on accessible public wall drops without 802.1X quarantine.
Contractor Warranty Fix:Administratively disable unassigned switch ports and enforce 802.1X Network Access Control.
